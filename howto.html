01

The core idea

This is an Age-of-Empires-like RTS in readability and progression, but it deliberately moves complexity upward into macro strategy instead of downward into constant worker and soldier babysitting.

Think: choose the economy shape, territory, age timing, formation doctrine and faction plan — then let the army act as formations rather than individual click targets.
02

Macro economy

Your workforce is allocated across Food, Wood, Stone and Gold at the sector level. You shift priorities rather than ordering each worker separately.

  • Food supports population and formations.
  • Wood supports buildings, logistics and some units.
  • Stone supports defenses and heavier development.
  • Gold supports advanced formations, progression and high-value faction plans.

Strategic sites can add extra income, so territory and economy are linked.

03

Formation-level armies

Units are raised as formations. Commands such as defend, secure territory, attack, relocate or planetary move operate on formations rather than requiring constant single-unit micro.

Readable combat roles

Line checks Mobile, Mobile closes on Ranged, Ranged pressures Line, and Siege breaks structures. These are soft bonuses rather than hard immunities, so positioning, cost and faction mechanics still matter.

04

Units, support buildings and upgrades

The current playtest factions grow from a small opening roster into four formations and five buildings. Later units/buildings unlock by age, so the opening stays readable while mid/late-game armies gain more composition choices.

  • Support buildings unlock the faction's compact upgrade tree and also do something useful on the map: armor, healing, speed or damage support.
  • Support formations remain normal Line/Ranged/Mobile units, but carry a small visible aura so they deepen composition without introducing another counter class.
  • Efficient Supply has two levels and improves total resource income.
  • Veteran Formations has two levels and improves formation health + damage.
  • Fortified Works has two levels and improves building health + defensive firepower.
  • Each current playtest faction has one signature upgrade that strengthens its existing faction mechanic instead of adding a new subsystem.
The whole normal tree is only three two-level lanes plus one signature upgrade. The intent is build identity, not a giant technology checklist.
05

Factions are not skins

Each civilization has its own founder, economy shape, formation rules, buildings, units and native faction NPC. The current playtest set uses Ironvale for defense, Greenwake for sustain, Ashwind for mobility/aggression and Prismkin for deterministic timing/rhythm.

Visit Faction Hall for the current roster, detailed statistics and native NPC behavior.

06

Skirmish lobby + AI seats

The lobby contains four seats by default. Any slot can be Human, Faction AI, Connected AI or Closed. That allows 1–4 player-side seats in the current lobby contract.

Connected AI

A connected model occupies a normal player-side seat. The design rule is that it receives the same visible battlefield information that a human in that seat would receive — no hidden map information just because the seat is controlled by AI.

Faction AI

This is different: every faction has its own native in-game NPC strategist. That strategist can control a faction in skirmish and can also appear as a reusable actor/brain inside authored Battle Maps.

07

World Domination

World Domination is a persistent real-time clan layer above the individual RTS maps. A match can be 1v1, 2v2, 3v3 or 4v4 on one larger territory globe.

  • You can only attack a territory directly connected to one your clan controls.
  • An expedition can only take formations physically stored on its source territory. Units from distant maps cannot teleport into the fight.
  • Every owned territory has cities, a local treasury, local resources and its own formation production.
  • Automatic production pays the faction's real Food/Wood/Stone/Gold unit cost. If that map cannot afford the next formation, production waits instead of creating free units.
  • The territory keeps an operating reserve. Only genuine surplus after local production costs flows into the clan treasury.
  • Other owned territories keep producing in real time while a border battle is taking place.
  • Every four successfully paid local formation productions create one bonus expedition-reserve formation. It must be assigned to an owned territory before it can join a future attack.
  • Territories have city objectives. Neutral territories begin with neutral cities; after conquest, city ownership becomes part of the new clan state.

The strategic globe already supports territory slots without requiring every battle map to exist today. Use the Map Editor to build maps later and attach them to territory nodes as the map library grows.

08

Defend the Workshop

This is the dedicated 1–4 allied-seat survival mode. Everybody shares one Workshop, one loss condition and one scarce Workshop Supply pool.

  • Passive supply begins deliberately slow.
  • Every cleared wave gives salvage and permanently increases passive supply gain.
  • The Workshop starts with two heavy Guard Towers and can buy more fixed-perimeter defenses.
  • Supply can also repair the Workshop or recruit formations from the active allied faction.
  • Every wave clear offers one of three run upgrades.
  • Every fifth wave is a heavier pressure wave.

Multiple human seats currently share one screen and use the active-seat selector for macro commands. Faction-AI allies can help autonomously, and Connected-AI seats have a command-event surface ready for a later model bridge.

09

Battle Maps

Battle Maps are our campaign-like mode, but they are primarily premade battle challenges, not a forced story campaign. A Battle Map can stage a defense, moving front, race for territory, limited-force assault, planetary control challenge or something stranger.

A Battle Map references a finished world map, then adds seats, factions, unit packs, starting formations, objectives, scene setup and rules.

10

Atlas Library

The Atlas is the player codex for things that exist in the game — faction and non-faction. It auto-builds entries from live game data for factions, founders, faction NPCs, units, structures, resources, terrain skins, decorations, scenario zones, maps, Battle Maps, content packs, game modes, upgrades and World Domination territories.

Imported unit packs, custom maps and imported Battle Maps can register local Atlas entries too.

11

The editors are intentionally separate

  1. Map Editor: build geography — flat map or globe, starts, terrain, resource regions and strategic locations.
  2. Battle Map Editor: pull in a finished map and content packs, then stage the actual battle challenge.
  3. Scenario Studio: advanced layer for deep triggers, variables, object rules, campaign metadata, surface skins and scripted scenario behavior.
World Domination territory nodes can reference those finished maps later. The strategic globe does not need to be rebuilt each time a new battlefield is created.
12

Globe conquest

Globe battles use actual spherical coordinates and great-circle movement. Armies can march over the horizon and fight around the far side of a planet. Surface paint can also carry gameplay properties such as movement multipliers or hazards.

Use Globe Conquest for the current spherical runtime and the Map Editor / Scenario Studio to author globe worlds.
