01

The core idea

This is an Age-of-Empires-like RTS in readability and progression, but it deliberately moves complexity upward into macro strategy instead of downward into constant worker and soldier babysitting.

Think: choose the economy shape, territory, age timing, formation doctrine and faction plan — then let the army act as formations rather than individual click targets.
02

Macro economy

Your workforce is allocated across Food, Wood, Stone and Gold at the sector level. You shift priorities rather than ordering each worker separately.

  • Food supports population and formations.
  • Wood supports buildings, logistics and some units.
  • Stone supports defenses and heavier development.
  • Gold supports advanced formations, research and high-value faction plans.

Strategic sites can add extra income, so territory and economy are linked.

03

Formation-level armies

Units are raised as formations. Commands such as defend, secure territory, attack, relocate or planetary move operate on formations rather than requiring constant single-unit micro.

Battlefield reading

Use the minimap, health bars, strategic-site ownership and faction mechanics to decide where to commit strength. Defensive structures can actively engage nearby formations.

04

Factions are not skins

Each civilization has its own founder, economy shape, formation rules, buildings, units and native faction NPC. For example: Northpole rewards founder-centered preparation, Suitcase rewards movement, Fatfrotz rewards mass, and Clockwork rewards precise set formations.

Visit Faction Hall for the current roster and its native NPC behavior.

05

Skirmish lobby + AI seats

The lobby contains four seats by default. Any slot can be Human, Faction AI, Connected AI or Closed. That allows 1–4 active seats in the current lobby contract.

Connected AI

A connected model occupies a normal player-side seat. The design rule is that it receives the same visible battlefield information that a human in that seat would receive — no hidden map information just because the seat is controlled by AI.

Faction AI

This is different: every faction has its own native in-game NPC strategist. That strategist can control a faction in skirmish and can also appear as a reusable actor/brain inside authored Battle Maps.

06

Battle Maps

Battle Maps are our campaign-like mode, but they are primarily premade battle challenges, not a forced story campaign. A Battle Map can stage a defense, moving front, race for territory, limited-force assault, planetary control challenge or something stranger.

A Battle Map references a finished world map, then adds seats, factions, unit packs, starting formations, objectives, scene setup and rules.

07

The editors are intentionally separate

  1. Map Editor: build geography — flat map or globe, starts, terrain, resource regions and strategic locations.
  2. Battle Map Editor: pull in a finished map and content packs, then stage the actual battle challenge.
  3. Scenario Studio: advanced layer for deep triggers, variables, object rules, campaign metadata, surface skins and scripted scenario behavior.
This keeps map builders from needing to understand campaign logic just to make a good battlefield.
08

Globe conquest

Globe battles use actual spherical coordinates and great-circle movement. Armies can march over the horizon and fight around the far side of a planet. Surface paint can also carry gameplay properties such as movement multipliers or hazards.

Use Globe Conquest for the current spherical runtime and the Map Editor / Scenario Studio to author globe worlds.
